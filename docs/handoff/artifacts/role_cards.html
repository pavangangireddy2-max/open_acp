AI Engineer Ladder

Career framework for content-department domain teams · August 2026 · titles follow the AI Engineer – [Domain] Learning Systems pattern · five levels, from a 6-month internship to the org seat · editable master: role_cards.xlsx (8 tabs)
How to read the cards: every progression area and rating line is wired to named KPI rows — anything in a mono chip is a live row on the KPI tracker or the team view, so reviews read off the sheets instead of impressions. Areas with no chip say so by design. Every transition has a written gate, every rung a stay bar, and the A1–A4 Agent Scope scale grades the agent work; comp for the changed rungs sits with HR.
Production principle: all content production runs through agents — humans design, review, and improve the systems that produce. Named exception, so the claim stays honest: the video production pipeline (recording, editing, review) — agenticity not required there. This is what makes the first two rungs’ job descriptions true, and it turns Agentic Production Coverage from a metric into a mandate.

The ladder

Five levels, linear — an internship rung, then four employee rungs; AI Engineer 3 sits above Lead (org-wide scope) and the progression matrix below deliberately stops at Lead. Comp: Lead and AI Engineer 3 keep inherited bands; the changed rungs are with HR — no invented numbers. Strip the comp line before wide sharing if needed.
Associate AI Engineer – [Domain] Learning Systems
Internship stipend — set by HR | 6-month internship
6-month internship — manages agents under supervision from day one: runs existing pipelines, reviews outputs, tunes prompts, handles escalations (A1), then shows measurable improvement on an agent's numbers (A2). Produces representative content items manually during the ramp — the domain floor. Converts through the gate below.
Answers for: Contributes on sample surfaces along the internship ramp — no rows answered for; growth is read through ramp evidence (A1→A2 on the Agent Scope scale).
AI Engineer – [Domain] Learning Systems
9–24L, band under HR review (the full IC span) | ≥1 year in role before Senior eligibility
Manages agents. Owns modules end-to-end and builds new content agents — 2–3 built and adopted by year-end, impact visible in the metrics (A3). One band, covering the full individual-contributor span before Senior.
Answers for: Answers for the Section B velocity + quality rows of the modules they own (their content hours, pieces, issue-recurrence share) — a topic surface of ≈ 100 topics at FullStack / GenAI complexity, refreshed every 6 months, with 2–3 content agents built and adopted (A3).
Senior AI Engineer – [Domain Portfolio] Learning Systems
Band under HR review — new rung | reached through the 2× gate below
Manages agent systems and mentors humans — the force-multiplier rung. Holds ≈2× an AI Engineer's complexity-weighted surface by depth, breadth, or leverage; orchestrating systems of agents (A4) is the leverage route. Invents new ways to teach with agentic AI and proves them with rigorous measurement.
Answers for: Answers for a domain slice ≈ 2× an AI Engineer's complexity-weighted topic surface — by depth, breadth, or leverage (A4 agent systems); first Section C asks raised in their name.
AI Engineer Lead – [Domain Portfolio] Learning Systems
24L to 30L | 5+ years
Manages humans who manage agents. Runs the team: full Section B answered for at review; people outcomes — ratings, retention, growth, hiring — define the title. Span ~5–8 members (number with HR).
Answers for: Answers for the team's full Section B at review and supports the Section A lane numbers; span ~5–8 members; the rating framework below applies as written.
AI Engineer 3 – [Domain Portfolio] Learning Systems
30L+ | 7+ years
Example: "AI Engineer 3 – Portfolio (All Domains) Learning Systems". Sets org-wide content and curriculum strategy. Owns EdTech infrastructure evolution, GenAI content pipelines, and cross-product technical roadmap.
Answers for: Shapes org-tracker rows and cross-domain standards; portfolio spans teams — reads through §5 department KPIs and org KRAs, not one team view.

Agent Scope — the A1–A4 scale

Role levels say what you answer for; the A-scale grades the agent work itself. The gates and stay bars below cite it.
ScopeWhat it means
A1 — OperateRun an existing agent pipeline: review outputs, tune prompts, handle escalations; keep accuracy and cost inside budget.
A2 — ImproveMove an agent's numbers: evals, retrieval quality, unit-cost reduction — with a before/after you can show.
A3 — BuildDesign and ship a new content agent end-to-end whose impact holds in the metrics. AI Engineer bar: at least 2–3 built and adopted.
A4 — OrchestrateSystems of agents carrying production: multi-agent workflows, escalation design (when agents act alone, when they hand to humans), agents other people run.
Role mapping: Intern converts having shown A1 + A2 · AI Engineer reaches A3 within the year · Senior operates at A4 (the leverage route) · Lead runs the team's agent fleet through people · AI Engineer 3 sets the org's agent architecture.

Promotion gates

Each transition has a written gate — all requirements, plus who proposes and who confirms. These blocks become the standing Progression Policy document when formalized.
Conversion gate — Associate AI Engineer → AI Engineer, at 6 months
  1. Runs assigned agent pipelines end-to-end without supervision — prompts tuned, outputs reviewed, escalations handled (A1–A2 shown).
  2. Judgment demonstrated: the responsible reviewer — their mentoring AI Engineer or Senior — confirms the intern's reviews catch what their own review would catch, sampled on real work through the ramp; they can tell good output from plausible output.
  3. Domain floor: has produced representative content items manually during the ramp — you can't review what you can't do.
  4. Decided by: supervising Senior / AI Engineer Lead proposes with ramp evidence; HOD confirms. Package set by HR at conversion.
AI Engineer → Senior AI Engineer — the 2× bar
  1. Surface ≈ 2× an AI Engineer's — courses × complexity, any mix of depth, breadth, or leverage (A4 agent systems are the leverage route).
  2. Quality held: all their Section B rows at budget, 2+ consecutive cycles.
  3. Leverage visible: Agentic Production Coverage contribution plus falling cost rows for their courses.
  4. People growing: mentorship evidence (Power Performers Created) — guiding work, not owning ratings.
  5. AI Engineer Lead proposes with row evidence; HOD + calibration confirm. Minimum 1 year in role.
Senior AI Engineer → AI Engineer Lead — people outcomes define the title
  1. A Lead seat exists — the span rule creates them: ~5–8 members per Lead (number with HR).
  2. Already answering beyond their slice: full-Section-B literacy shown at reviews, Section A lane awareness.
  3. People outcomes proven as a mentor: retention and growth of the people they guided.
  4. Accepts the accountability that defines the title: ratings, retention, growth and hiring for members.
  5. HOD + PM-head calibration confirm.
AI Engineer Lead → AI Engineer 3 — the org seat
  1. Org-level surface already held: org-tracker rows shaped, cross-domain standards authored and adopted.
  2. Their team runs without them day to day — the bus-factor test, passed.
  3. By org need, not tenure. HOD proposes; founders / PM-head calibration confirm.

Holding the role — the stay bars

The gates say how you climb; this says what keeping the seat means. One rule under every bar: your rung's eight scored rows at budget — 100%, no governance breach. Below budget more than twice in 12 months breaks the bar; two consecutive cycles below starts a structured gap conversation with your Lead — what's missing, the plan, the timeline. A conversation, not a demotion. The tables below are those eight rows, per rung — the same grid the merit rating scores: four output rows, four governance rows, 12.5% each.
How to read the gate column: Mandatory = the rung's defining requirement · Breach blocks = a breach zeroes that 12.5% slice and blocks eligibility for the cycle · ⚑ = a number not locked yet (a proposed default, adjustable).
Domain chips — build complexity sets the topic count behind the same ≈ 200 CWT per cycle: Low 1.0× — English · Aptitude · Mathematics Medium 1.5× — Programming · CS Core · DevOps High 2.0× — FullStack · GenAI Very High 2.5× — System Design · DS & Algo · DS / ML · low-churn marks the two slots that swap on low-refresh domains (note below).

AI Engineer 3

Reads through department KPIs and org KRAs, not one team view — the bar stays directional until those budgets land: the standards they authored still adopted and alive · portfolio Section B healthy across teams · a Lead bench ready behind them · places the org's learning-systems bets.

Low-refresh domains (English, Aptitude, Mathematics, Programming, CS Core, DS & Algo, DevOps, System Design) run the same bar with two occupants swapped: Tech Stack Freshness → Learning Systems Design impact · Business Impact → Pedagogy Initiative Impact. The refresh audit still runs — 100% of the surface audited each cycle, rebuilt where the audit calls.

Cost bars scale with domain complexity, and every number here is adjustable as real costs land: an objective practice item (FIB, MCQ, MMCQ — any type) ≤ ₹3 · a coding question under ₹100 baseline — ~₹200 where a question is effectively a project (FullStack, GenAI), up to ₹300 where it ships editorials, brute-force and efficient solutions (DS & Algo).

CWT — complexity-weighted topics: topic count × the domain complexity multiplier. ≈ 200 CWT per AI Engineer per 6-month cycle (= ≈ 100 topics at FullStack / GenAI 2.0×); a Senior holds ≈ 400.

Topic surface — how a rung’s load is argued: a topic is PPT + recorded video session + practice content + quiz content, so a domain’s catalogue hours are ≈ its topic count. Every topic is refreshed on a 6-month cycle and planned as a from-scratch rebuild. That puts one AI Engineer’s surface at ≈ 100 topics of FullStack / GenAI complexity per cycle, and a Senior’s at ≈ 2× that, complexity-weighted — the cards write this as CWT (complexity-weighted topics: topic count × the domain multiplier, ≈ 200 per AI Engineer, ≈ 400 for a Senior). Lighter-refresh domains — English, Aptitude, programming fundamentals, CS Core, Mathematics, DS & Algo — carry roughly double the topic count, and those seats are weighed harder on Learning Systems Design and Production Systems: refreshing costs less there, so invention and agent building are where the load belongs. Read on the team view as Tech Stack Freshness Rate.
